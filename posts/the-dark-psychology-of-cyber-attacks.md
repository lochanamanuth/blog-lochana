# The Dark Psychology of Cyber Attacks: An Empirical Analysis of Psychological Triggers in Phishing Success Rates

**Author:** Lochana Manuth  
**Date:** February 18, 2026  

**Abstract**—As technical security measures become more sophisticated, adversaries are increasingly pivoting toward human-centric exploitation. This paper investigates the efficacy of psychological triggers-specifically Urgency, Curiosity, and Authority-within a controlled phishing simulation. Utilizing a cloud-hosted infrastructure (InfinityFree) and real-time telemetry (Google Analytics), the study evaluated a sample of 30 participants. Results indicate that Urgency-based manipulation yielded an 80% Click-Through Rate (CTR), significantly outperforming other emotional triggers. This research highlights the critical necessity of integrating behavioral science into modern cybersecurity defense strategies.

**Keywords**—Phishing, Social Engineering, Behavioral Cybersecurity, Cognitive Bypass, Human Vulnerability

---

## I. INTRODUCTION

In the current cybersecurity landscape, the 'Human Element' is often characterized as the most vulnerable vector in the security chain. While cryptographic protocols and perimeter defenses provide robust technical barriers, Social Engineering exploits the biological and cognitive biases inherent in human decision-making. This study explores the 'Cognitive Bypass' phenomenon, where emotional stimuli override rational skepticism, facilitating successful phishing attacks despite increasing public awareness of cyber threats.

## II. METHODOLOGY

* **A. Technical Infrastructure:** The simulation was hosted on InfinityFree, leveraging a PHP-backend to handle redirection and session management.
* **B. Data Acquisition:** Google Analytics was utilized for telemetry, capturing real-time interaction metrics and engagement duration.
* **C. Experimental Variables:** The target demographic ($n=30$) was divided into three cohorts:
  * **Group A (Urgency/Fear):** Messaging focused on immediate account suspension.
  * **Group B (Curiosity/Greed):** Messaging offering exclusive rewards or free data.
  * **Group C (Authority/Trust):** Messaging impersonating official institutional policies.

## III. RESULTS AND ANALYSIS

| Trigger Type | Samples ($n$) | Successes (Clicks) | CTR (%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Urgency (Fear) | 10 | 8 | 80 |
| Curiosity (Greed) | 10 | 5 | 50 |
| Authority (Trust) | 10 | 3 | 30 |

The 80% CTR in Group A suggests that perceived threats create a sense of panic that effectively disables analytical thinking (System 2 thinking), leading to impulsive action (System 1 thinking). Group C's lower success rate (30%) indicates a growing skepticism toward traditional authority-based digital requests among the 18-25 age demographic.

## IV. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION

The data confirms that technical patches are insufficient against psychological exploitation. The 'Dark Psychology' of cyber attacks relies on leveraging universal human emotions to bypass advanced security protocols. To mitigate these risks, cybersecurity education must evolve to include psychological resilience training, teaching users to recognize and pause during high-stress emotional triggers.
